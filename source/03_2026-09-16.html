Race developments

  • DR In Maine, former Sen. Joe Manchin crossed party lines to endorse Sen. Susan Collins (R) over challenger Troy Jackson, calling her "the soul of the Senate" in a CNBC interview.

    Portland Press Herald & The Hill, Sept 14 — pressherald.com

  • R Also in Maine: the National Federation of Independent Business endorsed Collins at a Naples campaign stop, citing her PPP-loan and constituent-service record. Collins confirmed she skipped Trump's Dallas midterm convention to keep local campaign events instead.

    Maine Morning Star, Sept 14 — mainemorningstar.com

  • R In Georgia's Senate race, Gov. Brian Kemp formally endorsed Rep. Mike Collins over Sen. Jon Ossoff (D), and noted national Republicans had unsuccessfully tried to recruit Kemp himself for the race. Both campaigns confirmed for an Oct. 8 televised debate.

    WRDW, Sept 14 — wrdw.com

  • D Ossoff and Collins held dueling events in the Athens area: Ossoff, joined by former Atlanta Mayor Keisha Lance Bottoms, cast his opponent as a "Trump puppet," while Collins toured a nursing program with Dr. Mehmet Oz.

    WRDW, Sept 14 — wrdw.com

  • R Trump publicly reaffirmed his endorsement of Republican Darren Bailey against Gov. JB Pritzker (D) in Illinois — even as Bailey has been quietly distancing himself from Trump to court moderate suburban voters.

    Chicago Sun-Times, Sept 14 — suntimes.com

  • D Trump traveled to Gastonia, NC on Sept 16 to rally for Senate nominee Michael Whatley, whom local coverage notes trails Roy Cooper (D) in both fundraising and polling.

    WRAL, Sept 11 — wral.com

  • Arizona's two most competitive House seats sharpen: AZ-01 (Adam Feeley, R, vs. Amish Shah, D) and AZ-06 (Rep. Juan Ciscomani, R, vs. Kirsten Engel-successor Cesar Mendoza, D), both DCCC/NRCC top targets, following a Sept. 10 debate.

    KOLD, Sept 14 — kold.com

03

Election administration & security

  • The Supreme Court rejected the Trump administration's request to lift lower-court injunctions blocking new USPS rules that would have required states to submit voter lists and use new tracking barcodes on mail-ballot envelopes. The majority left the injunctions in place; Justices Alito and Thomas dissented and would have granted the stay; Justice Kavanaugh concurred separately, saying there wasn't enough time to implement the change before the midterms regardless of the merits. Effect: mail-ballot handling proceeds unchanged for 2026.

    NPR, Sept 14 — npr.org

  • Separately, the fight over DHS's "SAVE" voter-citizenship database (DHS v. League of Women Voters, No. 26A308) remains unresolved: the League of Women Voters coalition's opposition brief was due to Chief Justice Roberts by 4pm ET on Sept 15. As of this writing, no ruling, stay, or order has been reported by SCOTUS — still pending, worth checking again tomorrow.

    SCOTUSblog / Law & Crime, Sept 8–9 (docket) — scotusblog.com

04

Worth sharing

"No last-minute rule changes to how your ballot gets delivered." That's the plain-English version of yesterday's SCOTUS ruling — and it held even with a Trump-appointed justice (Kavanaugh) declining to grant the stay. Voting by mail is already underway in several states; this keeps the rules from shifting under voters mid-stream.

Same story as above — NPR, Sept 14 — npr.org
