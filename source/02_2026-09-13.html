Race developments

Movement in competitive Senate, House and governor races.

Senate · NH

New Hampshire sets its Senate matchup: Sununu vs. Pappas for Shaheen's open seat

In the September 8 primary, Trump-endorsed former Gov. John E. Sununu won the Republican nomination over seven rivals, including former Massachusetts state senator Scott Brown. Democratic Rep. Chris Pappas beat democratic-socialist challenger Karishma Manzur for the Democratic nod. The two will compete for retiring Sen. Jeanne Shaheen's seat — a race Republicans see as their best shot at a New England pickup, in a state that hasn't elected a GOP senator since 2010.

Al Jazeera · Sept 9, 2026 — aljazeera.com

Texas · Polling

Bipartisan poll finds sharp Hispanic-voter drop-off from Trump's 2024 Texas coalition

A Cook Political Report/TelevisaUnivision poll of Texas Hispanic voters, conducted by Democratic pollster Hart Research and Republican pollster OnMessage Inc. (Aug 26–Sept 3), found Trump's job approval at just 33% among this group despite their strong 2024 support for him. Democratic candidates led 2-to-1 in both the Senate and governor races among Hispanic respondents, and Democrats held a 53%–39% generic-ballot edge in the Rio Grande Valley/South Texas — the region behind marquee House races in TX-15, TX-28 and TX-34.

Cook Political Report · Sept 10, 2026 — cookpolitical.com

Polling

Iowa poll: Republican Hinson leads Senate race; Democrat Sand leads for governor

An Emerson College/Nextstar Media poll of Iowa (Aug 31–Sept 1, ±3.6 pts) found a split picture on the same ballot: Republican Ashley Hinson led Democrat Josh Turek 50%–45% for the open Senate seat, while Democrat Rob Sand led Republican Zach Lahn 49%–46% for governor. Pollsters noted roughly 6% of respondents were splitting their ticket across the two races.

Emerson College Polling / Nextstar Media · Sept 8, 2026 — kcrg.com

Litigation

DHS asks Supreme Court to revive citizenship-screening voter database; response due Sept 15

DHS filed an emergency application on September 8 asking the Supreme Court to let it resume using an overhauled version of the SAVE database — originally built to verify immigration status for benefits applicants, now expanded to cross-check voter rolls using Social Security Administration data. A federal district court found in June 2026 that adding SSA data violated privacy law, and a federal appeals court declined earlier this month to lift that block. DHS says the system has verified citizenship for over 65 million voters across 26 states while flagging 28,635 people as potential noncitizens — a figure the agency has not made public methodology for, and comparable DHS claims in other states (including a Nevada figure revised from 15,903 down to 185, reported in this digest's first issue) have previously been walked back after challenge. The Supreme Court has ordered voting-rights groups to respond by September 15; the database remains blocked until then.
