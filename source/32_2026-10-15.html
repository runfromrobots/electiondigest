Election administration & security

Court ruling — just surfaced

A federal judge vacated DOJ's entire nationwide voter-roll data program. On Oct 9, U.S. District Judge Sparkle Sooknanan (D.D.C.) ruled for Common Cause that the Justice Department's "list maintenance" effort — demanding unredacted voter rolls from nearly all 50 states and checking them against the federal SAVE immigration database to flag people it considered ineligible — exceeded DOJ's authority under the Civil Rights Act of 1960, the NVRA, and HAVA. She vacated the policy nationwide under the Administrative Procedure Act, including for the roughly 18 states that had already complied, and barred DOJ from using that data or sharing it with DHS. She wrote the policy amounted to "the federal government's attempt to decide who gets to vote," a decision Congress left to the states. DOJ said it's reviewing the ruling; separately, federal law bars systematic roll purges within 90 days of a federal election, which may limit the practical effect before Nov. 3.

Flagging this now rather than on the day it happened: this ruling fell outside this digest's normal 24–48-hour window and was missed by every run since Oct 9 — it only turned up in a broader search today. It is reported on the day found, consistent with how this digest handles belatedly-discovered developments.
Sources: Al Jazeera, Oct 9 · Democracy Docket, Oct 9
