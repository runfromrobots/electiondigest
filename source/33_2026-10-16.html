1 / Race developments

Senate, House & governor races — recruitment, fundraising, debates, endorsements.

Georgia Senate: Ossoff and Collins trade their sharpest attacks yet in final debate

In their second and likely final matchup (Atlanta, Oct. 13), Ossoff pressed Collins on his son-in-law's white-nationalist social media posts and whether he'd stop more from his home; Collins countered by asking why Ossoff won't return a megadonor's Epstein-linked money. Collins declined to say whether Biden legitimately won 2020 or whether he'd back ground troops in Iran; Ossoff repeated he'll serve a full six-year term and won't run for president in 2028. Both candidates still back the Laken Riley Act.

Maine Senate: Collins and Jackson's third debate lands as a low-energy draw

Coverage of the final Bangor debate (Oct. 13) called it repetitive of the earlier two. Collins put distance between herself and the administration — she didn't vote for the "One Big Beautiful Bill" and said Trump "did not have a good plan" on Iran — while agreeing with Jackson on data-center costs and heating oil. The sharpest moment was Collins accusing Jackson of impugning her integrity; Jackson referenced an unexplained FBI-investigation claim (which Collins denies) but never landed a clean attack.

Kansas Senate: Trump plans a rally for Marshall as the seat turns into an unplanned battleground

Trump is expected to campaign for Sen. Roger Marshall as part of an October swing through roughly ten states, though no date is set yet. Cook rates the race a toss-up — notable for a seat that normally isn't competitive at all; Kansas hasn't elected a Democratic senator in nearly a century.

4 / Worth sharing

Georgia just shattered its own early-voting record — verified by the Secretary of State's office

On the first day of early voting (Tuesday, Oct. 13), more than 180,000 Georgians cast ballots — over 40,000 more than on the first day of the 2022 midterms (about 135,000), a roughly one-third increase. The state says it has "a good chance" of topping 2022's full-cycle total of more than 1 million early votes. Absentee-ballot requests have already reached 130,000, nearly half of 2022's full-cycle total of 286,000, with weeks of early voting still to go.

Why it's worth texting: this isn't a poll or a vibe — it's a hard, official count straight from the state's election office, in one of the most-watched Senate and governor battlegrounds on the map.
