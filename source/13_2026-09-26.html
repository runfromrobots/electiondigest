IA Senate Turek rolls out fuel-cost plan; ethics complaint filed against Hinson; Ernst endorses Hinson Sept 25

Democrat Josh Turek proposed pausing diesel exports, suspending the federal gas tax, and opening strategic oil reserves to address fuel prices. Republican Ashley Hinson called for Congress to reconvene over fuel costs and the Iran conflict, texting Speaker Johnson to push for a return to D.C., but declined to commit to a war-powers vote. The two traded sharp personal criticism on the trail. Separately, the PAC Defend the Vote filed an ethics complaint alleging Hinson gave her husband special congressional access (she calls it politically motivated), and Sen. Joni Ernst endorsed Hinson at a state GOP dinner.

Source: Iowa Capital Dispatch, Sept 25

House · Rating change Cook Political Report: 15 House race ratings shift toward Democrats Sept 25

Cook moved 15 individual House race ratings toward Democrats, headlined "GOP on Track to Lose House Majority." New aggregate: Democrats favored in 208 seats, Republicans in 205, with 22 toss-ups (17 held by Republicans, 5 by Democrats) — Democrats need a net gain of just 3 seats for the majority. Cook's own analysis attributes the shift to Trump's underwater approval (particularly on the economy) dragging down GOP candidates in districts he carried, partly offset for Republicans by new Democratic-favorable maps in California and Utah against GOP redistricting gains elsewhere. Cook says Democrats are positioned to gain 5–15 seats, for a likely 220–230 seat majority.

Source: Cook Political Report, Sept 25 — corroborated with matching seat counts by Axios and The National Desk, Sept 25

3 Election administration & security

Factual, sourced reporting only — no speculative "stolen election" scoring.
SCOTUS Supreme Court lets DHS's SAVE voter-verification database proceed for now Sept 25

The Supreme Court issued an emergency stay lifting a lower federal court's June order that had blocked use of the revamped SAVE (Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements) database, which the Trump administration wants states to use to check voter eligibility ahead of the midterms. This is a temporary stay, not a final ruling — the underlying case continues in the lower courts, and plaintiffs say the fight isn't over. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson dissented, joined by Justices Kagan and Sotomayor, warning that "legitimate voter registrations could be mistakenly revoked." Election-law expert David Becker told NPR the near-term practical effect is likely limited: state use of SAVE remains voluntary, federal law already bars most systematic voter-roll purges within 90 days of an election, and DHS has itself acknowledged the underlying data isn't perfect.

Sources: NPR, PBS NewsHour, Sept 25
