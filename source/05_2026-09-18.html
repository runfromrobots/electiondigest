01

Race developments

Competitive Senate, House, and governor races — what actually moved.

Michigan Senate — A new Emerson College poll (fielded Sept 12–14) has Abdul El-Sayed (D) at 48% and Mike Rogers (R) at 46%, with 4% undecided — a 2-point edge for El-Sayed, inside the ±3-point margin of error. It's the fourth or fifth public poll in a row to show this race essentially tied, regardless of which side leads by a nose.

Senate
Emerson College Polling, Sept 17, 2026 · also covered by Washington Post

Oregon Governor — A second straight independent poll shows Republican Christine Drazan narrowly ahead of Democratic Gov. Tina Kotek: DHM Research (fielded Sept 3–9) had it 43–41 Drazan on the initial ask, 45–43 after leaners, inside a ±4-point MOE. That's the poll that prompted Cook Political Report's move on this race from Likely D to Lean D (logged in the Sept 13 issue) — a real erosion for Democrats in a state they've held since 1987.

Governor
Oregon Public Broadcasting · Sept 15, 2026

Record congressional exodus — NPR's retirement/defeat tracker now counts 99 House and Senate seats guaranteed to have a new member after 2026 (83 House, 16 Senate) — close to one in five lawmakers from the start of this Congress won't be back, a turnover level NPR says is rivaled only by 1992. Newest name: Sen. Amy Klobuchar (D-MN), now presumed to be running for governor instead of re-election, alongside Sen. Marsha Blackburn (R-TN) doing the same.

Election administration & security

Factual, sourced reporting only. No speculative rigging or "stolen election" scoring — that isn't something this digest quantifies.

SAVE voter-database case now fully briefed at the Supreme Court — The League of Women Voters and EPIC filed their opposition brief Sept 15, completing briefing in DHS v. League of Women Voters, the fight over the Trump administration's bid to revive its overhauled SAVE citizenship-screening database for use against state voter rolls. SCOTUSblog reports the court's decision "could come at any time." No ruling yet as of this issue; the lower-court injunction blocking the database remains in effect in the meantime.
