01Race developments

NC & GA Senate — national GOP money reportedly pulling back

Politico reported the NRSC, chaired by Sen. Tim Scott, is deprioritizing North Carolina and Georgia and shifting ad spending toward Iowa, Texas, Maine and Alaska instead. Whatley downplayed the report, saying he's spoken with Scott and Majority Leader Thune and "will get the support we need to win this race." Cooper continues to hold a significant fundraising edge over Whatley. This follows Thursday's reporting (covered here Sept 27) on GOP anxiety and ad-reservation cuts in both states.

Texas Governor — race tightens in the polling average

Abbott's lead over Democrat Gina Hinojosa has narrowed to 1.8 points (47.8%–46%) in the RealClearPolitics average; Hinojosa has led two individual polls, though Abbott remains favored overall. Abbott dismissed the tightening ("Polls don't win elections, and polls are often wrong"). Hinojosa has accepted four televised debate invitations; Abbott has accepted none, saying he prefers direct voter contact.

Ohio Governor — Ramaswamy and Acton trade fire in a toss-up open seat

The race centers heavily on Acton's record as state health director during Ohio's COVID-19 response, a live vulnerability with conservative voters ("There's just no way in the world I would vote for Amy Acton," one Columbus-area electrician told NPR). Both campaigns have launched aggressive attack ads. Ramaswamy and Acton agree, notably, on curbing data-center growth and cutting property and gas taxes.

PA-7 & PA-10 — new district poll shows Democrats narrowly ahead

A Franklin & Marshall poll has Democrat Ashley Ehasz-Brooks leading Rep. Ryan Mackenzie 45%–40% in PA-7, and Democrat Janelle Stelson leading Rep. Scott Perry 47%–44% in PA-10 — both within the margin of undecideds. Both are seats Cook already rates competitive.

03Election administration & security

Voter-roll data fight: states say they're winning at trial

As early voting opens in some states, Illinois election officials say Illinois and roughly 30 other states remain in federal court resisting the administration's demand for complete, unredacted voter-registration databases. "We've won at the trial court level — 25 of those cases. That's going to go on for a while," said Illinois State Board of Elections spokesman Matt Dietrich. This is a separate legal track from the DHS SAVE-database case already covered here (SCOTUS stay, Sept 25).

Scoring methodology

Score counts only confirmed race-rating changes from a named nonpartisan rater (Cook Political Report, Sabato's Crystal Ball, etc.) — never polls, fundraising, endorsements, or aggregate probability/generic-ballot numbers. Each individual race movement is a flat ±1 (+1 toward Democrats, −1 toward Republicans) regardless of how many notches it crossed; a day's score is the sum of that day's qualifying moves. Court rulings, including redistricting decisions, do not score on their own.
