01Race developments

NC SENATE

GOP's North Carolina retreat widens to three states

Axios reports the Senate Leadership Fund has redirected $17 million it had reserved for Michael Whatley into Kansas, Texas, and Alaska instead — a wider pullout than Tuesday's reporting suggested. Republicans still outspent Democrats in North Carolina through September ($53M to $44M) before the shift. A GOP operative told Axios Whatley "took one for the team"; his campaign says it's confident it has the resources to win regardless.

OH SENATE

Trump stumps for Husted in Ohio; fact-check finds most claims false

Trump rallied with Jon Husted in Vandalia on Oct. 3, a sign the race is close enough to warrant a presidential visit. PolitiFact's review of the event's claims found Husted's charge that Sherrod Brown gave undocumented immigrants Social Security benefits and free healthcare rated False, and Trump's "almost no inflation" line rated Misleading (August inflation was 3.4%). One claim held up: Ohio's 3.3% August unemployment rate is a genuine record low.

PolitiFact · Oct 3, 2026 — politifact.com
SPECIAL ELECTIONS

Democrats now 13-for-13 flipping red state legislative seats

Ballotpedia's running tally finds Democrats have flipped 13 previously Republican-held state legislative seats in special elections since Trump's second term began in January 2025, with Republicans flipping zero Democratic-held seats in the same stretch. The wins (3 in Mississippi, 2 each in Florida, Iowa, and Pennsylvania, plus one each in Arkansas, Georgia, New Hampshire, and Texas) cost the GOP its supermajorities in the Mississippi and Iowa state senates. For comparison, Democrats flipped 19 seats over the full two years of Trump's first term.

03Election administration & security

ELECTION PREP

Democrats' Election Day playbook, and where Trump has left the door open

A CNN rundown of Democratic preparations for Nov. 3 describes: a Senate election-protection task force Schumer has run since April; a bipartisan Senate observer program led by Sen. Alex Padilla monitoring polling sites through certification; House Democrats' regional hearings on Rep. Joe Morelle's list of "more than 150 possible threats"; the Elias Law Group litigating 85 voting-law cases in 41 states; and a $25 million ACLU effort training roughly 10,000 volunteers. On the other side of the ledger: Trump, asked whether he'd deploy ICE or the National Guard at polling places, said he'd do "anything necessary" for honest elections. Former press secretary Karoline Leavitt said in February that ICE at polling stations was "not something I've ever heard the president consider." Federal law already bars armed federal agents from polling places, and no court has authorized any such deployment; Trump and allies have "declined to rule out" one. Democratic congressional leaders have filed suit seeking to block it preemptively.

04Worth sharing

A perfect record: 13 flips, 0 losses

Since Trump's second term began, Democrats have flipped 13 Republican-held state legislative seats in special elections — and lost exactly none of their own. It's a clean, nonpartisan box score (Ballotpedia keeps the running tally) that doesn't need spin: the GOP has lost its supermajority in two state senates along the way, and the pace roughly matches the 19-seat wave that preceded the 2018 blue wave.

Why it's shareable: it's a hard number, not a poll — and numbers like this are what eventually show up in November turnout.
